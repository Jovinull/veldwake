# ADR-0004: Rigid voxel character parts and analytical locomotion

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-09-17
- Owners: Veldwake maintainers
- Supersedes: None
- Superseded by: None

## Context

M5 has to produce a humanoid that belongs to the M4 region, generated by systems rather than authored in a modelling tool, and it has to do so without a physics engine, an animation graph, an asset pipeline, or a rigging step. Two decisions in that space outlive the milestone, because everything built on top of them — equipment, second archetypes, crowds, combat poses — inherits their consequences.

The constraints that force a decision are the ones already written down. [`STYLE_BIBLE.md`](../audiovisual/STYLE_BIBLE.md) requires voxel structure to stay visible and intentional. [`ADR-0003`](0003-procedural-first-audiovisual-production.md) requires content to be produced by systems. [`ADR-0002`](0002-presentation-independent-authority.md) requires the authoritative representation to be testable without a renderer. The audited host is an integrated GPU whose frame budget M4 already spends on terrain.

The two questions are: how a generated body deforms, and where its animation comes from.

## Decision

**Bodies are rigid voxel parts, one per bone, and nothing is skinned.** A compiled character is sixteen small dense voxel volumes, each meshed once by the existing exposed-face mesher and each drawn with one transform. Animation writes transforms; it never touches geometry. Every non-root part's volume extends at least one voxel past its joint toward its parent, so no rotation can open a hole, and that overlap is asserted against compiled volumes rather than hoped for.

**Locomotion is analytical and driven by distance, not by clips and not by time.** Joint angles are closed-form functions of a gait phase, the phase advances with distance travelled, and the blend between idle, walk and run is a function of speed expressed in leg lengths per second. Ground contact is a two-bone analytical IK solve against a `GroundSampler` that returns the top face of the topmost solid voxel — a step function, matching the blocks a viewer can see, not a smoothed height field.

The boundary: this covers one humanoid archetype standing, walking and running on terrain. It is not an animation system. Layered animation, transitions with state, additive poses, procedural secondary motion, and anything that needs a clip are out of scope and are not prejudged by this ADR.

## Alternatives considered

- **Skinned meshes with weighted vertices.** Standard, expressive, and the wrong shape for this game: skinning shears the voxel grid, which is the one thing the style bible forbids, and it would require per-frame vertex work on a GPU that has none to spare. It also needs a rigging step, which is an authoring tool by another name.
- **A physics engine (Rapier or similar) for contact and ragdoll.** Rejected for M5. The contact problem here is "which block is under this foot", which a sampler answers exactly and a solver answers approximately; adding a dependency, a fixed timestep, and a second source of truth for where a body is would buy nothing this milestone needs. Revisit when something needs dynamic response, not before.
- **Authored or purchased animation clips.** Directly contrary to ADR-0003, and they would make the character the one part of the game that cannot be generated from a seed.
- **Time-driven animation with a separate foot-locking pass.** Simpler to write and wrong at every speed change: a cycle driven by time slides the feet whenever speed and cadence disagree, and the lock then fights the animation. Advancing phase with distance makes stride length the thing that is specified and removes the failure entirely.
- **Sampling the continuous terrain height field for contact.** Mathematically smoother and visually wrong: a sole placed on a smooth surface either floats above the block it stands on or sinks into it. The image is what the milestone is judged on.

## Positive consequences

- Geometry is static: a character is compiled once, uploaded once, and never re-uploaded. The per-frame cost is one small uniform per part.
- The whole domain is testable without a GPU. Poses, contacts, joint limits, reach and determinism are asserted headlessly, and a locked fixture signature makes an unintended change to any of them a failing test.
- Voxel structure survives every pose, because no voxel ever moves relative to its part.
- A body of a different size gets the same gait at its own speed, because the thresholds are leg-relative.

## Negative consequences

- Joints are visibly hard. Two rigid boxes rotating about a shared joint read as a mechanical elbow at close range, and the overlap that keeps them closed is a permanent voxel cost — it is what makes the sleeve one voxel narrower than the forearm, which M5 answers with a material rule rather than with geometry.
- No squash, stretch, or secondary motion is possible without adding parts.
- Analytical gaits do not compose. A second gait is a second set of closed-form curves and a second blend, not a clip dropped into a graph, and the number of hand-tuned constants grows with the number of behaviours.
- Contact is a per-foot solve against a step function, so a planted foot crossing a terrace edge follows the new block over a few frames rather than staying pinned. Pinning a foot for the length of a stance is the standard cure and is deliberately not in M5.

## Future implications

- Equipment and second archetypes are additional parts and additional descriptor fields; neither requires revisiting this ADR.
- The first behaviour that genuinely needs blended, interruptible, stateful animation — combat, hit reactions, ragdoll — is the review trigger. At that point the choice is between growing the analytical layer and adding an animation system beside it, and this ADR should be superseded rather than stretched.
- A physics engine becomes worth reconsidering when something must respond dynamically to force. Terrain contact alone is not that thing.
